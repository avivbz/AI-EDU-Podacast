### 1. Integrating Generative Artificial Intelligence in Science Education: An Investigation of Primary School Teachers' Perceived Readiness and Views on Their Teaching Practices

1. **Title:** Integrating Generative Artificial Intelligence in Science Education: An Investigation of Primary School Teachers' Perceived Readiness and Views on Their Teaching Practices
2. **Publication date:** 2026-11-26
3. **Institution / publisher / platform:** Education Sciences 16(12), 2028 (MDPI)
4. **Author(s):** Alexandros Batzos; Giorgos Peikos; Anastasios Zoupidis; Angelos Markos
5. **Author note:** The authors are early-childhood and primary education researchers at the University of Western Macedonia and Democritus University of Thrace, Greece, working in science education and research methods.
6. **URL:** https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7102/16/12/2028
7. **Summary:**
This convergent mixed-methods study, framed by the Technology Acceptance Model, examined how ready Greek primary teachers feel to use generative AI in science teaching. A questionnaire was completed by 319 respondents (207 pre-service or recently graduated teachers and 112 in-service teachers), and 18 teachers were interviewed.

Respondents held moderately positive attitudes and rated their own competence as moderate, but described their training as insufficient and were neutral about available resources. They saw generative AI mainly as a help for lesson planning and preparing materials, and few mentioned uses or risks specific to science.
8. **Main conclusions / central claims:** The authors conclude that readiness is uneven, with attitudes ahead of training and support. They identify lack of training, accuracy concerns, overdependence by teachers and students, weak infrastructure and missing guidelines as the main barriers, and call for professional development on science-specific, critical and ethical use of generative AI.
9. **Validity:** Peer-reviewed journal article; mixed methods (survey n=319, interviews n=18); non-probability sample and self-reported data.

### 2. Investigating the effectiveness and boundary conditions of generative AI scaffolding in science game-based learning

1. **Title:** Investigating the effectiveness and boundary conditions of generative AI scaffolding in science game-based learning
2. **Publication date:** 2026-11-26
3. **Institution / publisher / platform:** Education and Information Technologies 32, 3 (2027) (Springer Nature)
4. **Author(s):** Yulin Gong; Minkai Wang; Wei Li; Hanjie Gu; Haihua Peng; Huijuan Chi; YuCheng Qian
5. **Author note:** The team combines education researchers from Minnan Normal, Northeast Normal, Zhejiang and Zhejiang Shuren universities with teachers from Jiaxing Experimental Middle School, China.
6. **URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10639-026-14188-6
7. **Summary:**
In a quasi-experiment, 80 middle-school students (40 per group) played a science game set in a virtual factory. One group received dynamic scaffolding generated by generative AI and the other received traditional static scaffolding. The study measured achievement, flow, motivation and cognitive load, and logged how often students interacted with the AI.

The generative-AI group scored higher on achievement, flow and motivation, with no difference in cognitive load between groups. More frequent interaction with the AI was associated with higher achievement.
8. **Main conclusions / central claims:** The authors conclude that generative AI scaffolding can support situated science learning in games. Exploratory subgroup comparisons suggest the benefit is concentrated among students with low prior knowledge, which the authors say needs formal testing with larger samples.
9. **Validity:** Peer-reviewed journal article; quasi-experimental design (n=80); summary based on the abstract and landing page.

### 4. What jobs can AI not do? I asked the machines

1. **Title:** What jobs can AI not do? I asked the machines
2. **Publication date:** 2026-11-25
3. **Institution / publisher / platform:** Slow AI (Substack)
4. **Author(s):** Sam Illingworth
5. **Author note:** Illingworth is an academic who writes the Slow AI newsletter on critical, deliberate use of AI and frequently addresses AI in education.
6. **URL:** https://theslowai.substack.com/p/jobs-ai-cannot-do
7. **Summary:**
Illingworth asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude which jobs should never be handed to AI. ChatGPT listed 20 professions such as surgeons, judges and pilots, Gemini named five areas, and Claude listed the author's own university duties, including marking, external-examiner decisions and viva outcomes, which he suspects reflected files in his account.

He observes that the lists of protected roles leave out the drafting, summarising, screening and first-round feedback that fill many working days, that ChatGPT offered to do exactly those tasks, and that its lists changed across four repeated runs.
8. **Main conclusions / central claims:** The author argues that chatbot answers about AI-proof work are unstable and tilted toward high-status roles, and that everyday knowledge work, including academic marking and feedback, is more exposed than such lists suggest. His advice section is paywalled.
9. **Validity:** Blog post based on informal prompting; partly paywalled. Relevant to assessment work in higher education; K-12 relevance is indirect.

### 7. Understanding secondary school students' attitudes toward artificial intelligence in education

1. **Title:** Understanding secondary school students' attitudes toward artificial intelligence in education
2. **Publication date:** 2026-11-25
3. **Institution / publisher / platform:** Discover Education 5, 1387 (Springer Nature)
4. **Author(s):** Snježana Dobrota; Josipa Jurić
5. **Author note:** The authors are teacher-education researchers at the Faculty of Humanities and Social Sciences, University of Split, Croatia.
6. **URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44217-026-02457-y
7. **Summary:**
The study surveyed 1,394 secondary students from 47 Croatian schools in the 2025/26 school year, using Croatian versions of two AI-attitude scales. It examined how attitudes vary by gender, school type, grade, achievement, city size, parental education and frequency of AI use.

Students were most positive about bringing AI into the education system, and ChatGPT was the most used tool (about 77% of students). Attitudes differed across demographic factors, but frequency of AI use showed the strongest association with attitudes.
8. **Main conclusions / central claims:** The authors conclude that frequency of use matters more than socioeconomic factors in shaping students' attitudes toward AI, and describe the results as preliminary because neither scale's original structure fitted the Croatian data and the sample was a convenience sample.
9. **Validity:** Peer-reviewed journal article; cross-sectional survey (n=1,394).

### 8. Developing and validating the AI-REFINE process to support pre-service science teachers in co-designing STEM lesson plans with generative AI

1. **Title:** Developing and validating the AI-REFINE process to support pre-service science teachers in co-designing STEM lesson plans with generative AI
2. **Publication date:** 2026-11-25
3. **Institution / publisher / platform:** Discover Education 5, 1390 (Springer Nature)
4. **Author(s):** Do Huong Tra; Nguyen Quang Linh
5. **Author note:** The authors are science-education researchers associated with Thai Nguyen University of Education, Vietnam.
6. **URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44217-026-02480-z
7. **Summary:**
The authors developed AI-REFINE, a six-step human-in-the-loop process for co-designing STEM lesson plans with generative AI, and had nine experts validate its content in a two-round Delphi study, which produced a high content validity index.

They then piloted the process for six weeks with 28 pre-service science teachers. Co-design scores rose from about 5.8 to 7.8 out of 10 and every participant improved. The main difficulties reported were spotting subtle AI errors and time pressure.
8. **Main conclusions / central claims:** The authors present AI-REFINE as a content-valid framework with preliminary promise, and state that without a control group its causal effectiveness is not established and controlled trials are needed.
9. **Validity:** Peer-reviewed journal article; Delphi study plus single-group pre/post pilot (n=28).

### 9. Pre-service teachers' pedagogical decision-making with Generative AI in lesson planning

1. **Title:** Pre-service teachers' pedagogical decision-making with Generative AI in lesson planning
2. **Publication date:** 2026-11-25
3. **Institution / publisher / platform:** The Australian Educational Researcher 54, 16 (2027) (Springer Nature)
4. **Author(s):** Sun Yee Yip; Jeffrey Ji; Lynette Pretorius; Mihye Won (author list may be incomplete on the landing page)
5. **Author note:** The authors are teacher-education researchers at Monash University, Australia.
6. **URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13384-026-01039-z
7. **Summary:**
Eighteen second-year Bachelor of Education students each wrote a lesson plan, generated a second plan with generative AI, and then compared and revised the two. They were interviewed afterwards, and the interviews were analysed thematically through a teacher-agency lens.

Three patterns of interaction emerged: teacher-centred traditionalists, selective collaborators and emerging strategists. Which pattern a student showed was shaped by prior experience and beliefs about teaching.
8. **Main conclusions / central claims:** The authors report that responsibility for pedagogical decisions stayed with the pre-service teachers in every pattern, and that their ability to evaluate AI output depended on their existing content and pedagogical knowledge. They recommend building structured verification and reflection into teacher education.
9. **Validity:** Peer-reviewed journal article; qualitative interview study (n=18) at one university.

### 11. Human-AI Collaboration in Indonesian Language Writing Instruction: Effects on High School Students' Creativity, Critical Thinking, and Writing Originality

2. **Publication date:** 2026-11-25
3. **Institution / publisher / platform:** Jurnal Pendidikan Terapan (JUPITER) 4(5), 733–740
4. **Author(s):** Anita Candra Dewi; Sakinah Fitri
5. **Author note:** Both authors are at Universitas Negeri Makassar, Indonesia.
6. **URL:** https://journal.diginus.id/JUPITER/article/view/2504
7. **Summary:**
In a sequential explanatory mixed-methods study, 72 tenth-graders at a state high school in Makassar (36 per group) were taught Indonesian-language writing either with human-AI collaboration or conventionally. ### 15. From naïve certainty to intellectual humility: how an AI-SSI curriculum reshapes high school students' socioscientific orientations

2. **Publication date:** 2026-11-24

### 18. Educators' use of artificial intelligence to prepare and implement inquiry tasks in primary education

### 19. How teachers refine and enact culturally situated STEAM worksheets drafted by generative AI for learning matrices in lesson study

1. **Title:** How teachers refine and enact culturally situated STEAM worksheets drafted by generative AI for learning matrices in lesson study
2. **Publication date:** 2026-11-23
3. **Institution / publisher / platform:** Research Square (preprint; under review at Discover Education)
4. **Author(s):** Muhamad Galang Isnawan; Naif Mastoor Alsulami; Lalu Sirajul Hadi; and four further authors not named on the landing page
5. **Author note:** The team works on mathematics lesson study in Indonesia; affiliations were not shown on the landing page.
6. **URL:** https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-11355632/v1
7. **Summary:**
This design-based study followed worksheets on matrices, drafted by generative AI and set in local Indonesian cultural STEAM contexts, through teacher refinement and four lesson-study cycles with 36 Grade 11 students.

Only 6 of 48 prompt specifications were fully met by the AI drafts. Students remained active, but their written justifications were thin, and some misconceptions and badly rendered notation had to be corrected. Pre/post gains were large, and the class outperformed a comparison class (n=35) that took only a post-test.
8. **Main conclusions / central claims:** The authors conclude that the usefulness of AI-drafted materials depended on teacher refinement and enactment, and that because of the weak comparison design the results show the materials are compatible with learning, not that they caused it.
9. **Validity:** Preprint, not peer-reviewed; design-based research with a weak comparison design.
